Photosynthesis turns light energy into chemical energy. It happens in the chloroplasts of plant cells.

The light-dependent reactions take place in the thylakoid membranes. They split water, release oxygen, and produce ATP and NADPH.

The Calvin cycle takes place in the stroma of the chloroplast. It uses ATP and NADPH to fix carbon dioxide into sugar.

Cellular respiration breaks glucose down to release energy. Most of the ATP is made in the mitochondria.

Glycolysis is the first step of cellular respiration. It happens in the cytoplasm and splits glucose into two pyruvate molecules.

The Krebs cycle runs in the mitochondrial matrix. It releases carbon dioxide and produces NADH and FADH2.

The electron transport chain is in the inner mitochondrial membrane. It uses NADH and FADH2 to make most of the cell's ATP, and oxygen is the final electron acceptor.

Mitosis produces two identical daughter cells. Its phases are prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase.

Meiosis produces four genetically different cells with half the number of chromosomes. It is used to make gametes.

DNA is made of nucleotides. Each nucleotide has a sugar, a phosphate group and one of four bases: adenine, thymine, guanine and cytosine.
